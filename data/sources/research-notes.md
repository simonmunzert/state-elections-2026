## Added 1 Oct 2026: Will high mobilisation hold? (literature brief)

Own data (script 05): across 226 state-election pairs since 1946, the turnout change at the next election
regresses on the current change with slope −0.17 (p = 0.009): about one sixth of a surge is given back.
After surges of 7+ points (35 cases), the mean next change is −2.4 points and 62% of cases keep more than
half the gain. The 2019 East surges (SN +17.3, BB +13.4, TH +12.2) were followed by further rises in 2024
(+7.9, +11.6, +8.7); MV 2016 +10.4 → 2021 +8.9 → 2026 +7.3. Counter-examples: Saxony-Anhalt 1998 (DVU surge,
+16.9) → 2002 −15.2; Brandenburg 2009 → 2014 −19.1; Berlin 2021 → 2023 (repeat election) −12.4.

Individual-level habit formation:
- Gerber, Green & Shachar 2003 (AJPS): GOTV experiment; voting once raises next-election turnout
  (persistence parameter ~0.5; complier effects 39–42 pp).
- Coppock & Green 2016 (AJPS): experiments + voting-age RD in 17 US states: voting in 2008 raised 2010
  turnout by 9–15 pp and 2012 turnout by 12 pp; effects persist 20+ years with little decay; habit is
  election-type specific (presidential → presidential strongest).
- Fujiwara, Meng & Vogl 2016 (AEJ Applied): election-day rain shocks: 1 point lower past turnout →
  0.6–1.0 points lower turnout four years later (aggregate carry-over 0.6–1.0).
- Meredith 2009 (QJPS): voting-age RD: ~5 pp per cycle. Denny & Doyle 2009 (AJPS): state dependence ~13 pp
  after removing selection. Dinas 2012/2014: first eligible election is formative, 30-year persistence.
- Plutzer 2002 (APSR), Franklin 2004, Hobolt & Franklin 2011: habits set in the first ~3 elections;
  high-turnout elections leave a cohort footprint; competitiveness is the main driver of turnout.
- Counter-evidence: externally induced turnout fades: Bechtel, Hangartner & Schmid 2018 (compulsory
  voting in Vaud, +30 pp, back to zero after abolition); Garmann 2020 (German municipalities: concurrent
  high-office elections do not create persistent turnout unless the race was close); Jessen, Kühnle &
  Wagner 2021. Stakes-induced turnout sticks; pressure-induced turnout does not.

German evidence on mobilised non-voters:
- BTW 2017 (+4.6 to 76.2%): AfD mobilised precarious non-voter strongholds (Vehrkamp & Wegschaider 2017,
  Bertelsmann). Schäfer 2023 (in Schoen & Weßels): in 2021 the AfD did NOT disproportionately mobilise
  non-voters; AfD retention 2017→2021 ≈ 56%, 19% of 2021 AfD voters had not voted in 2017; turnout stayed
  at 76.4–76.6% (no reversion).
- BTW 2025 (82.5%, +6.2, highest since 1987): AfD +1.8 m from former non-voters (infratest/KAS);
  Vehrkamp & Posthofen 2026: AfD 2025 electorate = 38% loyal, 19% former non-voters, 41% switchers.
  Repräsentative Wahlstatistik 2025: turnout rose 7–8 pp among under-45s (21–24: 78.3%).
- Schulte-Cloos & Leininger 2022 (Party Politics; >10,000 municipalities 2009–2019): turnout surges
  benefit the AfD where political distrust is high, but depress it where disaffection is low.
- Schwander & Manow 2017: 2017 AfD gains came largely from previous abstainers. Micus (MV 2016): of ~150k
  remobilised non-voters, the AfD took ~55k.

Regression to the mean / polarisation:
- Campbell 1960 "surge and decline": peripheral voters drop out when the next election is lower-stimulus.
  US 2020 → 2024: 66.6 → 63.8%; UK 2017 → 2019: 68.8 → 67.3%. Germany federal 2013–2025:
  71.5 → 76.2 → 76.6 → 82.5, no reversion.
- Leininger & Meijers 2021 (Political Studies, 315 elections): turnout higher when populist parties are in
  parliament, significant in Eastern Europe; mobilises supporters and opponents. Immerzeel & Pickup 2015:
  PRR success mobilises educated opponents ("anti-populist mobilisation"). Cancela & Geys 2016 meta-analysis:
  closeness robustly raises turnout.

Slide-ready synthesis: habit effects of +5 to +15 pp per cycle are real and durable, strongest for
first-time voters and same-type elections; externally induced turnout fades, stakes-induced turnout
sticks; every East German surge since 2014 was followed by stability or a further rise; the AfD's
non-voter pull was strong in 2017 and 2025, absent in 2021, yet turnout never fell back. Expect
Saxony-Anhalt 2031 well above 60% unless the AfD-vs-rest frame collapses; a 2–5 point reversion is plausible.
